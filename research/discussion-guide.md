# Discussion guide — Comfort-Loop Listeners (45 min)

Companion doc to [segment-and-screener.md](segment-and-screener.md). Run this with
each of the 5-6 recruited respondents. Record (with consent) and take notes against
the template in [synthesis-template.md](synthesis-template.md) as you go, or
immediately after each session while it's fresh.

**Materials needed:** a working link to the Discovery Companion MVP for section 5
(local `http://localhost:3000/aura/companion` or the deployed Vercel URL — see the
main README's Deployment section), screen-share ready.

**Interviewer ground rules:**
- Ask "why" or "tell me more about that" at least twice per answer before moving on —
  the first answer is almost always a summary, not the reason.
- Never say "repeat listening," "comfort loop," or "discovery problem" until section 5.
  Let them describe the behavior in their own words first; feeding them the frame
  contaminates the rest of the interview.
- If they contradict something they said earlier, don't correct them — flag it in
  your notes as a tension to probe ("earlier you said X, just now Y — walk me through
  that").

---

## 1. Warm-up (5 min)

- Tell me about your relationship with music day to day — where does it fit into
  your life?
- What app(s) do you use to listen? How long has [Spotify] been your main one?
- In general, would you call yourself someone who actively seeks out new music, or
  someone who mostly listens to what you already know? *(no wrong answer — just
  establishes their self-image before behavior questions expose the gap)*

## 2. Map the last week of actual listening (10 min)

- Walk me through your listening yesterday, start to finish — every time you had
  music on, what were you doing, and what did you play?
- Now the day before that. *(getting two real days, not a generalization, is the
  point — generalized self-report is unreliable, specific recent recall is much less so)*
- Of everything you just described, which tracks/artists were ones you'd heard many
  times before? Which (if any) were new to you?
- When you did play something familiar — was that a deliberate choice, or did it
  just happen (autoplay, an old playlist, muscle memory opening the same tab)?

## 3. Root-cause probing (10-12 min)

This is the core section — the goal is to find the *mechanism*, not just confirm the
behavior exists.

- Think of the last time you deliberately went looking for something new. What
  triggered that? What did you do?
- What normally stops you from doing that more often? *(let them answer, then probe
  each of these if they don't surface it unprompted: time/effort, fear of a bad
  pick ruining the mood, decision fatigue from too many options, not trusting
  recommendations, discovery features feeling hidden or ignorable, social pressure —
  music playing for/around other people)*
- Tell me about a time [Spotify] recommended something new and it actually landed.
  What made that one work?
- Tell me about a time a recommendation missed. What happened right after — did you
  try again, or go back to something familiar?
- If you had a magic button that guaranteed your next new-music pick would be good,
  would you press it more often than you currently explore? *(tests whether the
  blocker is effort/trust vs. genuine preference for familiarity)*

## 4. Current discovery features + trust in the algorithm (8 min)

- What discovery features do you know about in the app today (Discover Weekly,
  Release Radar, Daily Mix, radio, etc.)? Which do you actually use?
- When you see a new recommendation, what goes through your head before you decide
  whether to play it?
- Do you feel like the app understands your taste? Where does it get it right, where
  does it get it wrong?

## 5. Concept test — Discovery Companion (10-12 min)

Frame it neutrally: *"I want to show you something we're exploring and get your
honest reaction — there are no wrong answers, and it's totally fine to tell me it
doesn't work for you."*

- Screen-share the Companion. Let them enter 2-3 real artists/genres they actually
  like and set the novelty dial themselves.
- Have them type a real request in their own words (not a suggestion chip) — ideally
  tied to something they mentioned in section 2 or 3.
- **Think-aloud while the response loads and renders:** "Tell me what you're
  thinking as you read this."
- Probe on:
  - Do the "why this fits you" explanations feel true, generic, or off?
  - Does the novelty dial concept make sense? Where would they set it normally?
  - Would they actually press play on any of these picks right now, today? Which
    one, and why that one specifically?
  - Compare directly: "How is this different from Discover Weekly / Daily Mix for
    you, if at all?"
  - What would make them trust this enough to use it weekly?
- Any friction, confusion, or skepticism during this section is signal — capture
  their exact words, not your paraphrase.

## 6. Wrap-up (3 min)

- If [Spotify] fixed exactly one thing about how you discover new music, what would
  it be?
- Anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Thank you + incentive logistics.
