# Example candidate profiles — FICTIONAL, for recruiting reference only

> **These are not real people and contain no real interview data.** They exist to help
> you recognize a good candidate while recruiting — a "does this sound like who I'm
> looking for" reference — and to sanity-check the screener in
> [segment-and-screener.md](segment-and-screener.md) against varied contexts. **They
> cannot be used as Part 2's deliverable.** The brief requires 5-6 real interviews;
> nothing below is a substitute for that.

Each profile shows what a real qualifying respondent's screener answers might look
like, spread across different life contexts so the screener's coverage gets a sanity
check before you use it on real people. No discussion-guide answers are included —
those only exist once someone real actually says them.

---

**1. Priya, 29 — marketing manager, Mumbai.** 3 years on Spotify, listens daily.
Primary contexts: commute, work focus. Self-estimated ~80% familiar listening.
Qualifying signal: *"I used to explore more than I do now — my Discover Weekly used to
be exciting, now I skip past it without really looking."*

**2. Marcus, 34 — software engineer, remote, Austin.** 5 years on Spotify, listens
several times a week. Primary contexts: work focus, workout. ~75% familiar, unsure at
first when asked. Qualifying signal: *"I don't really think about it, I just end up
replaying the same stuff."* (The screener flags this "unsure/autopilot" answer as
often the most interesting kind of respondent.)

**3. Aditi, 22 — grad student, Bangalore.** 2 years on Spotify, listens daily. Primary
contexts: social/parties, chores. ~70% familiar. Qualifying signal: *"I wish I
discovered more but don't get around to it — trying something new feels like a
gamble when I could just play what I know works."*

**4. Tomás, 41 — teacher, Manchester.** 6 years on Spotify, listens daily. Primary
contexts: commute, sleep/wind-down. ~85% familiar. Qualifying signal: *"I used to have
a much wider rotation. Now it's basically the same 40 songs on loop."*

**5. Grace, 26 — nurse, rotating shifts, Sydney.** 4 years on Spotify, listens several
times a week. Primary contexts: workout, chores/downtime. ~70% familiar, genuinely
unsure when asked to estimate. Qualifying signal: hadn't consciously noticed the
pattern until the screener question made her actually count.

**6. Daniel, 37 — small business owner, Toronto.** 8 years on Spotify (longest tenure
of the set), listens daily. Primary contexts: work focus, commute. ~90% familiar.
Qualifying signal: *"I know I'm in a rut. I keep meaning to explore, but by the time
I'm free to actually sit with music, I just hit play on the usual."*

## Using this for recruiting

- Post the screener from [segment-and-screener.md](segment-and-screener.md) somewhere
  your actual target segment hangs out (a Spotify-adjacent subreddit, a music-focused
  Discord, your own network) — these profiles are what a good response should sound
  like, not a script to hand out.
- Notice the spread: contexts, tenure, and *how* people phrase the qualifying signal
  all vary. If every real respondent you screen sounds identical to just one of these,
  that's worth noticing — it may mean your recruiting channel is biased toward one
  sub-type of the segment.
- Respondent #2 and #5 (the "unsure/autopilot" answers) are deliberately included
  because the screener calls that pattern out as often the most valuable kind of
  respondent — don't filter them out in favor of people with a tidier story.
